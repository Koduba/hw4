# Design: "Varsity Night"

**The idea:** Yale under stadium lights, in **Yale Blue**, styled like a college town at night: varsity lettering, felt pennants, a chenille patch and a scoreboard. The goal is a site that feels like a real campus shop, not a template, with the AI assistant ("Ask the Bulldog") built into the storefront rather than tucked away in a corner.

| Area | What changed | Why it helps people stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fonts** | **Graduate** (varsity block) for the logo, prices and labels; **Fraunces** (editorial serif, with italics) for headlines; **Inter** for reading. | It looks unmistakably collegiate at a glance, the kind of shop you'd trust for licensed Yale gear. Clear contrast between headline, label and body text makes pages quick to scan. |
| **Colour and texture** | **Yale colours:** deep navy backgrounds, **Yale Blue `#00356B`** on the crest, ticker and the shadow of the hero lettering, and **Yale light blue `#63AAFF`** for prices, links and buttons (navy text on top for strong contrast), with white for headlines. Two blue "floodlight" glows and fine film grain. | Reads instantly as Yale, which builds trust for licensed gear. The light blue always marks the next action (price, button, Ask), so the eye goes where buying happens. |
| **Brand** | A Yale Blue CC shield crest (logo, favicon, chat avatar), a Yale Blue scoreboard **ticker** of store facts, and a giant outlined **CAMPUS CUSTOMS** wordmark in the footer. | It feels like a real store with an identity, and states trust signals (officially licensed, 57 Broadway, XS–XXL) on every page. |
| **Home hierarchy** | A hero headline (*Wear your* / **BULLDOG** / *pride, everywhere.*), a **dorm-wall sticker collage** of real products with price tags and a "Boola Boola" chenille patch, a scrolling **college marquee**, "Fresh off the shelf" picks, felt **pennants**, a **scoreboard** of stats, and an assistant teaser. | The first screen shows real products and prices within seconds, and every section ends in a click toward a product. |
| **"Ask the Bulldog" bar** *(the innovative bit)* | A search-style bar in the hero that **sends your question to the AI assistant**. It opens the chat and lays the matching products out on the page (e.g. "navy hoodie in M under $70" → 17 cards). Idea chips and the collection pennants do the same in one tap. | Shoppers ask the way they'd ask a person in the store, and get a curated shelf instead of a 102-item list. |
| **Motion** | The varsity headline settles into place, stickers fall in and gently bob, the patch spins in, sections rise in as you scroll, and the ticker and marquee crawl (the marquee pauses on hover). A shine sweeps across product photos on hover. All of it turns off with *reduce motion*. | Feels alive and premium, and draws attention to products, without blocking anything or making anyone motion-sick. |
| **Product presentation** | Rounded white "shop window" tiles, a hover lift with light sheen, **cursor-follow zoom** on product photos, ticket-stub size buttons, a big light-blue varsity price, and an **"Ask the Bulldog about this"** button on every product page. | Customers can inspect embroidery and print up close, see the price instantly, and get a sizing or stock answer in one tap: fewer doubts before buying. |
| **Chat feel** | The panel is branded "Ask the Bulldog" with the crest avatar and a pulsing green "online" dot. Messages pop in. The launcher has a breathing blue ring and an "Ask the Bulldog" nudge on the Home page. | The assistant feels like a friendly shop clerk. The nudge invites first-time visitors to ask, which is where our size checks, alternatives and picks turn browsing into sales. |

**Kept on purpose:**
- Yale colours throughout (the site was first built in black and pink, then switched to Yale Blue on request);
- accessible contrast, focus rings and labels;
- everything responsive: on phones the hero stacks, grids drop to two columns, and nothing overflows sideways (checked at 390px).
